# Problem and product research

Read this reference when the problem, value, market context, or product direction is
uncertain.

## Establish the problem

- Identify the affected actor, current behavior, observable cost, and desired outcome.
- Distinguish user evidence from stakeholder belief and solution preference.
- Find current alternatives, including manual work or doing nothing.
- State what evidence would disprove the problem framing.

Use repository artifacts, support data, interviews, analytics, or primary external
sources that actually exist. Do not invent quotes, demand, or market size.

## Decide how much market work is needed

Market sizing and competitive research are optional. Use them only when they affect a
funding, positioning, pricing, or build-versus-buy decision.

When needed:

1. define the market boundary and time period;
2. show the calculation inputs and sources;
3. separate direct, adjacent, and substitute alternatives;
4. explain how the evidence changes the product decision.

Do not present unsourced top-down totals or call a category competitor-free.

## Record the result

Update the discovery specification with:

- problem and affected actors;
- observed evidence and source quality;
- desired outcome and non-goals;
- material alternatives and tradeoffs;
- assumptions, validation method, and blocking questions;
- the accepted product decision and why it follows from the evidence.
