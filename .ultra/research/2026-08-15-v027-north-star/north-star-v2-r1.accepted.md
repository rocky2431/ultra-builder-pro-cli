## Problem Reality

- Reality: an automatic coding workflow can produce locally plausible work while losing
  the owner's underlying purpose, crossing an effect boundary, or becoming impossible
  for another session or supported Host to resume and verify.
- Evidence: the accepted Change outcome and construction plan identify first-principles
  drift, model limits, cross-Host continuity, adversarial review, delegation authority,
  and recovery as load-bearing problems.
- Unknowns: v0.27 implementation and six-Host live drills remain delivery evidence; they
  do not become true merely because this direction is accepted.

## First-Principle Propositions

### FP-1 — Durable authority must outlive model context

- Proposition: work that must survive sessions and Hosts needs one owner-readable,
  versionable authority outside conversational memory.
- Evidence: `.ultra/project-brief.md`, `docs/ARTIFACT-AUTHORITY.md`, Git history, and the
  accepted Change recovery boundary.
- Causal consequence: canonical files plus Git carry intent, plan, evidence, and recovery;
  runtime observations remain reconstructable.
- Falsifier or revisit trigger: a real continuation drill cannot reconstruct the accepted
  boundary from canonical files and Git without hidden conversational state.
- Status: `accepted`

### FP-2 — Product meaning cannot be reduced to a mechanical signal

- Proposition: goals, causal claims, semantic completeness, trade-offs, and risk
  acceptance require model interpretation inside an owner-authorized frame.
- Evidence: the owner requires every development decision to return to first principles,
  while the accepted plan explicitly rejects scores, counters, and digests as semantic
  verdicts.
- Causal consequence: code validates exact structure, identity, permissions, effects,
  evidence, and recovery; the owner and model retain meaning and strategy.
- Falsifier or revisit trigger: an externally verifiable invariant is left unenforced, or
  a mechanical check begins accepting or rejecting a proposition's truth.
- Status: `accepted`

### FP-3 — Useful autonomy is bounded execution, not transferred authority

- Proposition: automatic coding is trustworthy only when scope, permissions, resource
  ceilings, owner gates, evidence, and recovery are explicit and inspectable.
- Evidence: the accepted Change outcome, execution-approval boundary, and `HC-2`, `HC-5`,
  and `HC-6`.
- Causal consequence: a Host model may choose and execute local tactics inside a live
  grant, but plan-critical changes and external effects return to the owner.
- Falsifier or revisit trigger: a fresh session can infer permission from stored files, a
  denied action has no retry/cancel/abandon path, or delegated work expands authority.
- Status: `accepted`

### FP-4 — Adversarial independence exposes shared blind spots

- Proposition: evidence-backed independent challenge is necessary because sequential
  self-review can preserve the producing model's assumptions and omit disagreement.
- Evidence: the owner's global adversarial-review requirement and the accepted lifecycle
  plan's six-lens, isolated-worker, and cross-family risk posture.
- Causal consequence: Research, Change, Plan, each task, Test, and Deliver preserve
  findings and disagreements for model or owner disposition.
- Falsifier or revisit trigger: independent review adds no material detection across
  representative evals, or its evidence is flattened into voting or a score.
- Status: `accepted`

### FP-5 — Portability requires shared semantics and native adaptation

- Proposition: one workflow meaning can travel across Hosts only when shared Skills stay
  host-neutral and each adapter absorbs native invocation, Hook, permission, and CLI
  differences.
- Evidence: the six supported Host surfaces and their different plugin, Hook, and
  headless execution contracts recorded in the accepted plan.
- Causal consequence: all six Hosts consume the same fourteen-Skill product while Doctor
  reports native activation and readiness honestly.
- Falsifier or revisit trigger: semantic behavior can be preserved only by Host-specific
  forks of a shared Skill, or lowest-common-denominator emulation blocks a native path.
- Status: `accepted`

### FP-6 — Delegation must preserve provenance and least authority

- Proposition: another CLI is an untrusted bounded worker whose inputs, writes, checks,
  identity, and returned delta must be bound to the exact authorized snapshot.
- Evidence: the accepted snapshot, secret boundary, exact-check brokerage, and no-auto-
  merge requirements.
- Causal consequence: dirty tracked work can be delegated without copying hidden inputs,
  granting canonical writes, or confusing a worker delta with the primary worktree.
- Falsifier or revisit trigger: a live drill copies an excluded secret or symlink, misses
  a snapshot race, runs an unapproved check, or integrates a stale delta silently.
- Status: `accepted`

## North Star Outcomes

### NS-01 — Recoverable owner-directed automatic coding across six Hosts

- Outcome: an owner can carry one real software Change from a first-principles Research
  baseline through bounded automatic coding, adversarial verification, cross-Host
  continuation, and recoverable delivery on Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Kimi Code,
  Grok Build, and ZCode.
- Observation method: validate canonical trace and recovery, exact candidate parity and
  readiness, lifecycle review evidence, bounded delegation receipts, and the relevant
  real-path drills against the accepted Change.
- Baseline: v0.26 provides file-first workflows and delegation, while the accepted v0.27
  Change records unresolved first-principles, autonomy, review, snapshot, and provenance
  closure; implementation evidence remains separate.
- Target or expected change: one exact v0.27 candidate satisfies the accepted Change
  criteria without an unauthorized commit, push, publication, deployment, provider
  purchase, or silent semantic reduction.
- Horizon: the v0.27 lifecycle-closure Change and its candidate acceptance.
- Anti-metric: do not optimize task count, automated-step percentage, finding count,
  validator success, or six green self-integrity checks while the real owner outcome,
  native activation, continuity, or recovery path fails.

## Hard Constraints

### HC-1 — Preserve six-Host semantic portability

- Protected value or threat: portable workflow meaning; the threat is Host divergence or
  lowest-common-denominator behavior.
- Constraint: Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Kimi Code, Grok Build, and ZCode receive the
  same fourteen-Skill product through host-native plugin or bundle surfaces.
- Authority or evidence: accepted Change scope and `FP-5`.
- Revisit condition: a supported Host loses a required native path or cannot preserve the
  shared Skill contract.

### HC-2 — Keep owner gates live and explicit

- Protected value or threat: owner authority; the threat is stored or inferred permission.
- Constraint: public workflows remain owner-invoked unless a current-session grant
  authorizes bounded continuation; North Star acceptance, material scope or risk changes,
  and external effects always return to the owner.
- Authority or evidence: accepted execution approval and `FP-2` through `FP-3`.
- Revisit condition: the owner explicitly changes the authorization model and accepts its
  recovery and effect boundary.

### HC-3 — Keep semantic authority file-first

- Protected value or threat: legible and recoverable project truth; the threat is hidden
  state or a duplicate semantic mirror.
- Constraint: project truth remains owner-readable files plus Git. Do not add an Ultra
  database, MCP kernel, semantic state machine, generated prompt projection, or daemon.
- Authority or evidence: accepted non-goals and `FP-1` through `FP-2`.
- Revisit condition: a reproduced primary-path failure proves files, Git, and native Host
  tools insufficient and the owner accepts a smaller necessary mechanism.

### HC-4 — Separate canonical meaning from derived observations

- Protected value or threat: one representation per semantic fact; the threat is stale
  caches, reviews, counters, or validators becoming authority.
- Constraint: every canonical artifact has a named writer, consumer, freshness rule, and
  recovery route; generated observations remain disposable and never author semantics.
- Authority or evidence: accepted artifact boundary and `FP-1` through `FP-2`.
- Revisit condition: an artifact's writer, consumer, freshness, or recovery contract can
  no longer be identified.

### HC-5 — Delegation grants no new authority

- Protected value or threat: source, secret, and integration control; the threat is an
  over-broad worker or an unbound returned delta.
- Constraint: delegated instructions, permissions, snapshot inputs, writes, checks,
  receipts, timeout, cancellation, and recovery remain exact and inspectable; workers do
  not write canonical `.ultra`, commit, publish, deploy, install, or auto-merge.
- Authority or evidence: accepted delegation boundary and `FP-6`.
- Revisit condition: a named provider cannot meet the exact snapshot and receipt contract,
  or a narrower native mechanism becomes available.

### HC-6 — Authorize every external effect separately

- Protected value or threat: accountable spend, credentials, supply chain, and external
  state; the threat is capability or readiness being mistaken for permission.
- Constraint: commit, push, tag, publication, deployment, real-HOME installation,
  authenticated provider calls, and incremental spend each require their applicable
  current owner authorization.
- Authority or evidence: accepted execution approval and `FP-3`.
- Revisit condition: an effect is proven reversible, internal, and already covered by an
  exact current grant, or the owner explicitly changes the boundary.

## Explicit Exclusions

- No Ultra-owned general conversation memory, code graph, deployment provider, framework
  guidance, persistent stage marker, workflow daemon, or new continue command.
- No mechanical truth, strategy, quality, finding-disposition, or risk-acceptance verdict.
- No automatic merge of delegated work, silent activation inheritance, or shared Skill
  fork whose only purpose is one Host's syntax.

## Uncertainties and Revisit Triggers

- Revisit `FP-4` when independent adversarial evals show whether native isolation or a
  cross-family probe materially changes defect detection.
- Revisit `FP-5` when a Host changes its plugin, Hook, permission, or headless contract.
- Return to Research when live evidence contradicts a causal chain, an anti-metric becomes
  the operational target, or the owner changes the desired outcome or protected value.
- Treat any accepted North Star replacement as a new revision: preserve old evidence,
  mark dependent Change traces stale, reconcile them explicitly, and never infer that the
  replacement approves execution.
